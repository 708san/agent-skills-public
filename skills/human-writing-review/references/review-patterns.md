# Review Patterns

Load this reference when diagnosing AI-like prose in detail, explaining review findings, or resolving a pattern that is not obvious from the core workflow.

Do not treat the categories as a mandatory checklist. Use only the patterns that materially affect the text.

## 1. Unnatural abstraction

Typical signs:

- broad nouns such as 「価値」「可能性」「本質」「重要性」「最適化」「体験」「課題」 without a concrete referent
- abstract nouns stacked together where a concrete actor or action would be clearer
- claims that could fit almost any company, product, or topic
- conclusions that restate a theme instead of saying what changes in practice

Preferred fixes:

- name the actor, action, object, condition, result, or example when supported by the source
- replace generic importance claims with the actual consequence
- delete abstractions that add no new meaning

## 2. Unnecessary points and padding

Typical signs:

- obvious setup the reader does not need
- repeated summaries of the same claim
- generic cautions or benefits added without relevance
- lists whose items overlap heavily
- closing sentences that only repeat the paragraph

Preferred fixes:

- remove the point when it does not advance understanding or a decision
- merge overlapping points
- keep only distinctions that change interpretation or action

## 3. Uniform sentence rhythm

Typical signs:

- repeated 「〜です。〜です。〜です。」 cadence
- paragraphs repeatedly using the same claim → explanation → summary shape
- repeated connective phrases such as 「また」「さらに」「一方で」「そのため」
- every point receiving equal length and emphasis

Preferred fixes:

- vary sentence length according to meaning
- combine sentences that belong together
- let important points breathe and compress minor ones
- remove connective words when adjacency already shows the relationship

## 4. Ambiguous logical relationships

Typical signs:

- cause and effect asserted without a visible mechanism
- examples presented as proof when they only illustrate
- contrast markers without a meaningful contrast
- conclusions stronger than the preceding support
- pronouns or subjects with unclear referents

Preferred fixes:

- clarify whether the relation is cause, condition, contrast, example, limitation, or conclusion
- weaken claims that exceed the supplied support
- specify subjects or referents when ambiguity matters

## 5. Generic comprehensiveness

Typical signs:

- balanced pros-and-cons coverage that dilutes the main argument
- generic “it depends” caveats after a useful conclusion
- unnecessary mentions of every stakeholder or scenario
- frameworks or numbered lists added mainly for completeness

Preferred fixes:

- align scope with the piece's actual purpose
- prefer a relevant omission over irrelevant completeness
- make priorities visible

## 6. Mechanical emphasis

Typical signs:

- repeated 「重要なのは〜です」
- repeated rhetorical questions
- excessive quotation marks, slogan-like fragments, or forced three-part lists
- repeated 「AではなくB」 contrasts without a meaningful distinction

Preferred fixes:

- reserve emphasis for genuine distinctions
- state the point directly when rhetoric adds no value

## 7. Weak specificity

Typical signs:

- phrases such as “many people”, “various situations”, “effective communication”, or “better results” without a concrete referent
- claims about users, teams, or markets without observable behavior
- advice that cannot be acted on because conditions are missing

Preferred fixes:

- add behavior, condition, consequence, or an example only when it is already supported by the source
- do not invent metrics, anecdotes, customers, evidence, or experience

## 8. Over-explaining and meta-commentary

Typical signs:

- 「ここで重要なのは」 before a plainly important point
- 「つまり」「言い換えると」 followed by a near-duplicate sentence
- 「以下では〜を見ていきます」 for an obvious or short section
- repeated explanations of what was just said or will be said next

Preferred fixes:

- trust the structure where possible
- keep signposting only when it reduces genuine cognitive load

## Impact ranking

When several patterns appear, prioritize them in this order:

1. meaning and logical clarity
2. unnecessary or repetitive content
3. abstraction and generic language
4. rhythm and sentence variation
5. minor wording polish

Group repeated symptoms under one root issue instead of producing a long issue list.
